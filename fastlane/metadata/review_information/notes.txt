Flags puts a country flag on an Apple Watch face, an iPhone Lock Screen, or a
Home Screen. There is no account, no sign-in, no purchase and no user-generated
content, so nothing is gated. Open it and everything is there.

PURPOSE AND AUDIENCE

You pick a country, and its flag goes in a widget or a watch complication. It
is for people who want a flag they care about somewhere they see it all day:
their own country while they live abroad, or a place they have a tie to. I
built it because I am Brazilian, I study abroad, and I wanted my flag on my
watch face.

HOW TO REACH EVERY FEATURE

No credentials, no sample files, nothing to set up.

1. Launch the app. The first screen is a grid of about 250 country flags,
   grouped by continent, with a search field.
2. Tap a flag. Its page shows the flag, its name and code, a star button, and
   the steps for each place the flag can go.
3. Watch face: press and hold the face, tap Edit, swipe to Complications, tap a
   slot, choose Flags, then pick the flag.
4. Lock Screen: press and hold it, tap Customise, tap the area under the
   clock, choose Flags, then pick the flag.
5. Home Screen: press and hold, tap Edit, tap Add Widget, search for Flags, add
   it, then touch and hold the widget to pick a flag.
6. Starring a flag keeps it at the top of the list, offers it in the watch's
   complication picker, and syncs it to the Apple Watch over iCloud.

The Apple Watch app has the same list and the same instructions.

Worth knowing while reviewing: in the widget gallery and the complication
picker, the preview next to "Flags" is a flat grey shape. That is WidgetKit
redacting placeholder content, not a broken widget. Configure it on a face or a
Lock Screen and the flag draws in full colour.

EXTERNAL SERVICES, TOOLS AND PLATFORMS

None. The app makes no network requests and has no server. All artwork ships
inside the binary. No analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no
authentication, no payment processor, no AI service, and no third-party SDKs or
dependencies beyond Apple's own frameworks.

Favourites are written to the user's own iCloud key-value store so they reach
their Apple Watch. That is Apple's iCloud and the user's account; we cannot
read it, and the app works with iCloud switched off.

REGIONAL DIFFERENCES

The app behaves the same everywhere, with one exception, which copies what the
operating system already does with the Taiwan flag:

- On a device set to China mainland the flag is not included, because Apple
  removes it from the system emoji font there.
- On a device set to Hong Kong or Macau it is not offered in the picker, which
  matches Apple hiding it from the emoji keyboard while still drawing it. A
  complication already configured with it keeps working.

No other flag is treated differently anywhere. The country list comes from the
operating system's region list, so it shifts with OS versions rather than with
storefronts, and country names are translated by the system.

THIRD-PARTY MATERIAL

The app is not in a regulated industry. The only third-party material is the
flag artwork, from flag-icons (https://github.com/lipis/flag-icons) under the
MIT licence, which ships inside the app beside the artwork. One flag, Saint
Helena, we assembled from flag-icons' Union Jack and a coat of arms on
Wikimedia Commons released into the public domain by its author. The app
holds national and territorial flags only: no club crests, badges or logos.
